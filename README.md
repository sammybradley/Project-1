# Fuelcast

## Author

Made by Sammy Bradley

## Description

Turn the numbers your fitness tracker already collects into a concrete plan: what to
eat, how much water to drink, when to do both, and the date you reach your goal weight.

Now a **React 19 + Vite** app. Recommendations remain rule-based — no runtime AI.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev       # dev server
npm run build     # production build
npm run preview   # serve the production build
```

No account, no server, no database. Everything you enter lives in that browser's
`localStorage` and never leaves the machine.

## What it does

| MVP behavior | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Enter a fitness / weight-loss / weight-gain goal | Setup screen — goal type, target weight, pace |
| Recommend meals, snacks and water to reach it | **What to eat next** card + water target in **Today** |
| Flag the optimal times to eat and drink | **Today's timing** card, with a live "now" line and *due now* markers |
| Enter activity and calories burned from a wearable | **Log → Activity**, plus a "straight from the tracker" panel |
| Show how close you are to the goal | **Goal & forecast** — progress bar, weight remaining, arrival date |
| Recommend day-to-day activity levels | **Move** card — weekly minutes, strength days, steps |

### Acceptance criteria

- **Estimated time in months and days.** The forecast reads e.g. *"2 months, 23 days —
  on track for November 24, 2026."* Once you have three logged days it also shows the
  arrival date implied by what you have *actually* been eating, which is usually the
  more honest number.
- **Meals researched, not invented.** The 50-item library carries real serving sizes
  and macros drawn from published high-protein and weight-gain meal guides (sources
  below), then a rule-based scorer picks from it for your goal, your open calories and
  your open protein.
- **Logging activity adjusts the day's intake goal.** Burn 400 kcal and today's target
  rises by exactly 400. Your tracker's *active calories* total already includes your
  workouts, so Fuelcast uses whichever source is larger — the tracker figure or your
  logged sessions — and never adds both.
- **Calorie intake resets at 12:00 AM.** Each day is stored under its own local calendar
  date, so at midnight the key changes and today's log is simply a new, empty record. A
  timer notices the rollover while the page is open and redraws with a notice; the header
  counts down to it. Past days stay on file for the 14-day history.
- **Consumption goals.** Calories, protein, carbs, fat and water, each with a target and
  a live bar.
- **Usable at 375 px and on a laptop.** One column on a phone, two from 760 px, three
  from 1180 px. Verified in-browser at both widths with no horizontal scroll.

## How the numbers are derived

Nothing here is guessed or generated by a model at runtime — it is all arithmetic in
`src/lib/calc.js`, and the tests in `test/engine-test.js` pin the results.

- **Resting metabolic rate** — Mifflin-St Jeor: `10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age + s`, where
  `s` is +5 male, −161 female, −78 if unstated. If your tracker reports resting
  calories, that number is used instead of the formula.
- **Maintenance** — RMR × a baseline factor for daily life (1.20 desk job → 1.70
  physical labour). The factors are deliberately low because logged workouts are added
  on top; this is what stops exercise being counted twice.
- **The daily gap** — 7,700 kcal per kg of body mass (3,500 per lb). Loss paces are
  0.35 / 0.6 / 1.0 % of body weight per week and gain paces 0.2 / 0.35 / 0.5 %, the
  bands the literature associates with holding on to lean mass. The plan never drops
  below your resting burn, or below 1,200 kcal (1,500 for men); if your chosen pace
  would, it is held at that floor and the forecast is recomputed from the slower rate
  you will actually achieve.
- **Protein** — 2.0 g/kg cutting, 1.8 gaining, 1.6 maintaining, measured against goal
  weight on a cut so a large deficit can't inflate the target. Fat is the greater of
  0.7 g/kg or 25 % of calories; carbohydrate takes the remainder.
- **Water** — ~33 ml per kg baseline, plus ~600 ml per hour of training you log.
- **Timing** — eating starts 30 minutes after you wake and stops about two hours before
  bed, with occasions spaced to land in the 3-4 hour band and at least 20-30 g of
  protein at each main meal. Hydration checkpoints run about every two hours and stop an
  hour before bed. The whole timetable shifts with the wake and sleep times in your plan,
  including an overnight shift pattern where bedtime falls after midnight.
- **Movement** — 150 minutes of moderate activity and 2-3 strength days a week, scaled
  up slightly for an aggressive cut. Calories for the "close the gap" suggestions come
  from MET values: `kcal/min = MET × 3.5 × kg / 200`.

## Layout

```
index.html               Vite entry — the legacy head, a root div, /src/main.jsx
src/main.jsx             boots React, imports the stylesheet
src/App.jsx              screens, banner, midnight rollover, card composition
src/styles.css           one stylesheet, light and dark, mobile-first
src/lib/data.js          50 meals and snacks, MET table, diet filters
src/lib/calc.js          the engine — pure functions, no DOM, no storage
src/lib/store.js         localStorage, day keys, history
src/hooks.js             useStore() and useClock() — store subscription, the tick
src/components/*.jsx     the setup form and each dashboard card
test/engine-test.js      89 assertions over the engine
```

Run the tests with:

```sh
npm test
```

Still `node test/engine-test.js` under the hood — no test framework. They cover the
formulas, the safety floor, the midnight rollover, the schedule generator (including a
bedtime after midnight), the recommender's ranking, and the export/import round-trip.

**Export / Reset** in the header write and clear the saved JSON, which is the closest
thing to a backup when there is no account to sync to.

## Deliberate limits

- **Notifications are in-page only.** The brief lists notifications as a non-goal, so
  Fuelcast does not use the browser Notification API, push, or the calendar. It marks the
  current slot *due now* on the timeline while the page is open.
- **No AI at runtime.** Recommendations are a scoring function over a fixed library.
- **One browser, one device.** No accounts and no cloud sync, by design. Clearing site
  data clears your plan — export first.
- **Not medical advice.** It is a class project built on population-level formulas.
